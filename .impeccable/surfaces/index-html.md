# Surface Brief: The Board

Scope: the kanban board (index.html) — the product's only surface. Visitor mode: Operate (the visitor completes a task; expression lives in precise details).

## Audience, job, proof

Solo developer/freelancer, often between meetings or commits, wants quick capture with no ceremony: see all work at a glance, move a card, never lose anything. Success = the board reads at a glance, drag feels mechanical and satisfying, and archive/restore is trusted. This surface doubles as the craft showcase the product is positioned on.

## Direction

The 8-Bit Atelier, the colored ditherpunk desktop (roll c33e5a42, one-bit desktop challenger, colored per user steer): columns are windows with accent title bars, cards are paper files, drag cuts a hole with marching ants and the drop line blinks. Board bench even-spaced until overflow, then left-aligned scroll.

## Entry surface

The same artifact carries a power-on homescreen: a live boot log announcing the real board, the four-square mark winking in steps, a wandering pixel mascot (idle, walk, jump, blink), and a blinking CLICK TO START. Click or any key enters; `?boot=off` skips it; the CRT collapse (steps) hands off to the desktop.

## Memorable moment

The first drag: a file is cut from its window, ants marching, and the target blinks a pixel line — the machine's one authored motion. The entry's moment is the mascot's jump as the screen powers down.

## Constraints

Product truth (archive/restore, filters, persistence, keyboard) stays intact through any restyle; the nine-pixel palette's fills must hold 4.5:1 with their ink/white text.
